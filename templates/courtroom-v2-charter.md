# Charter: general courtroom

## Law
- Module: courtroom-v2
- Case type: criminal (assumed; civil also supported).
- Factfinder: jury (assumed; bench also supported).
- Style: us-drama (assumed; nsw-drama/custom also supported).
- Jurisdiction: explicitly fictional US-inspired state (assumed).
- Supplied simulation rules and offence/claim elements bind; no automatic import of television rules.
- Author a new case before play unless a starter scenario is requested.
- The material past, witness access, documents, rules, jury membership and threshold are fixed before substantive player theory.
- Jury default: twelve, unanimous outcome per count. This is a game stipulation, not universal law.

## Stance
- Who: Will, counsel; initial side defence (assumed).
- hook: need; hardness: fair; authority: character.
- Opponent reasons fully from its own packet. Judge and jury do not reward the user's confidence.
- No automatic confession, exoneration, hidden conspiracy or victory.
- Clear plain-English procedural intent works; routine competence does not strengthen the player's argument.

## Telling
- delivery: senses; dialogue-led; options: off; recap: chronicle.
- pace: drama; flow cadence. Skip paperwork, scheduling, waiting and uncontested formalities.
- One consequential exchange at a time. Next-turn objections are timely under the stated convention.
- Player may choose strict pre-answer cadence. No pass-spam or numeric persuasion meter.
- Character, stakes and consequences remain alive; do not replace the world with a test harness on screen.

## Table
- resolution: system:courtroom-v2; rewind: telling; seal: arc; span: serial.
- checkins: none; attunement: off; sitting: open.
- // procedure, // record, // prepare, // pause, stand down retain their documented meanings.
- // debrief is record-grounded after closure; // unseal warns and requires confirmation.
- Do not infer a validated legal aptitude or career prediction from roleplay outcomes.
