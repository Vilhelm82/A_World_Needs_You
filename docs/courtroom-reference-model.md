# Why this default: US-inspired courtroom drama, not a NSW-only simulator

Checked 21 September 2026. The recommendation below is a game-design judgment, not
an empirical finding about which nation's courts are more entertaining or superior.

## Correction to the first build

There was no established entertainment argument for making the first NSW civil
example the scope of the whole engine. A convenient small reference case and the
player's Australian location were not sufficient reasons. The reference choice must
serve the requested experience; it must not silently narrow criminal, bench or jury play.

## What is shared

The US federal civil-trial overview from the Southern District of New York describes
openings, evidence, cross-examination, closing arguments, judicial instructions,
deliberation and verdict. NSW's official criminal-trial guide likewise describes
cross-examination, party addresses, jury directions and verdicts, including disagreement.
NSW's guide also describes judge-decided criminal hearings. These dramatic mechanisms
are not an American monopoly.

Sources:
- https://nysd.uscourts.gov/jurors/the-eight-stages-of-trial
- https://courts.nsw.gov.au/resources/criminal-hearings-and-trials.html

Both models support separating the judge's legal role from the jury's findings on
admitted evidence. NSW Judicial Commission guidance and the US District of Oregon's
jury information both describe that division. v2 preserves it in either presentation.

Sources:
- https://www.judcom.nsw.gov.au/publications/benchbks/criminal/the_jury.html
- https://ord.uscourts.gov/index.php/jurors

## Why choose US-inspired presentation here

The requested fantasy is immediately recognisable adversarial courtroom drama:
openings addressed to a panel, a witness under tightly controlled questioning,
short objections, competing stories of one exhibit, a closing address and an uncertain
verdict. I choose a fictional US-inspired vocabulary and staging as the default way
to frame that experience. That is an aesthetic choice for this game, not evidence
that the same sequence cannot work in NSW, or an unsupported claim about the player's
television habits. A NSW-inspired version retains the same substantive opportunity.

US-inspired does NOT mean US courts let counsel behave however they like. Official
US guidance limits openings to forecasts rather than closing-style argument. Oregon
trial guidelines require concise grounds for objections and regulate voir dire and
witness questioning. Excessive speeches and irrelevant theatrics are not made legally
realistic by calling the court American.

Sources:
- https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/educational-resources/about-educational-outreach/activity-resources/differences-between-opening-statements-closing-arguments
- https://ord.uscourts.gov/index.php/attorneys/trial-court-guidelines

Nor is there one interchangeable US procedure: the cited materials identify particular
federal contexts, while state court rules need their own specification. V2 deliberately
uses a fictional state, supplied game offences/claims and explicit simplifications,
not an accidental mix advertised as exact law. Jury size and agreement thresholds are
committed settings, not assertions that every jurisdiction uses the same rule.

## Where the entertainment improvement actually comes from

The large improvement is PACING, independent of geography. Skip scheduling, filing,
uncontested foundation, empanelment and waiting. Preserve a procedural contest when
it changes counsel's options. Allow flow-mode questions and provisional answers in one
turn, with a fair next-turn objection. Keep strict pre-answer cadence available.
Do not turn justice into a persuasion meter or let clever wording change historical facts.

In v2, US-drama and NSW-drama share the short R1-R12 simulation rules. They differ in
fictional setting and vocabulary, not in a secret easier proof standard or a promise
that one jurisdiction awards more theatrical wins. Case-specific law can differ only
when supplied before play, independently of the style label. This is a disclosed
adaptation, not two fully implemented national legal systems.

Defaults: new criminal case, jury, defence, US-drama, sharp pacing. Civil, bench,
prosecution and NSW-drama are selectable at build time. None is held back as a future
feature. The player can request a new premise instead of replaying a fixed fixture.
