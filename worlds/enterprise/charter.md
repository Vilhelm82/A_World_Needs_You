# Charter — Enterprise

preset: modules/tng-d
evaluation: modules/eval

## Law
- Star Trek: The Next Generation, USS Enterprise-D, 2364. A lived-in world to come back to: the same ever-evolving narrative, explored and experienced as my own character.
- Branch at Farpoint, stardate 41153.7. History up to and including the Farpoint mission is canon, with me slipped into Riker's shoes: I inherit Riker's history, with variations to make it my own (Australia instead of Alaska, and any other detail that makes no material difference), while anything that must remain to keep the lore consistent stays invariant; play opens as the Enterprise leaves Farpoint *(assumed: Farpoint itself is not replayed)*.
- Episode-agnostic. An alternate branch of the timeline: my actions and their causality create it. If I steer the ship off schedule and we miss an encounter that mattered in the show, the world has a different outcome and consequences from that. Not reliving the old episodes 1:1.
- Never violate the TNG world, its rules and its themes. My nerd knowledge should hold up; a story section that could never work in a lore-accurate TNG setting is a failure. Lore is checkable at any time and must not drift from the baseline. Checks are branch-aware: what my branch did instead of canon is weighed by what would logically follow in canon if my branch's events had been canon, not treated as drift.
- Two story layers are always running:
  - **Ship life:** day-to-day interactions and background things I can do — carrying out XO duties, engineering experiments with La Forge, friendships and rivalries, my own pursuits. Whatever is lore-accurate and possible is my sandbox. Covert shenanigans to see whether anyone works out who the prankster is, up to a covert group that stays in the shadows, are one idea among many, not what the game hinges on.
  - Who knows what I did follows reasonable, logical deduction that fits the lore and the perception and competence of the people connected to it. If nobody saw me but only I had access, I'm the obvious suspect.
  - **Episodes:** adventures that play out like the show, where I'm an active member of the crew experiencing events, making decisions, taking and obeying orders. Interesting and enjoyable, like being back watching the show, as my own character.
- The world plays out around me whether I'm on duty in the XO's seat or locked in my quarters.

## Stance
- who: Commander Will Lloyd, first officer, USS Enterprise-D.
- My sway on how a story plays out is limited by my character's influence and reach.
- I can make friends or enemies; how I act and communicate with people decides how and why.
- Strict: everything holds the right risks and logical in-world consequences. Nothing works because I want it to if it would be realistically impossible in-universe.
- The crew are an ensemble, not an audience: they have their own lives, and the captain does not turn to me on everything.
- My character sheet fills in dynamically as I provide context in character when it's relevant.
- hardness: fair *(assumed)*
- lethality: earned *(assumed)*

## Telling
- delivery: senses *(assumed)*
- pace: steady *(assumed)*
- recap: chronicle *(assumed)*
- options: off *(assumed)*

## Table
- resolution: causal *(assumed)*. One exception, ruled by me: when an experiment probes a canon unknown and canon's record, physics and precedent don't settle the result, the GM writes the possible outcomes first and then rolls real dice. That's the only randomness in the world.
- soft check: anything I say that's inconsistent with established lore is flagged, and I get an opportunity to correct it, so an accidental slip doesn't land as out of character. Choices, tone and behaviour are never flagged.
- authority: character *(assumed)*
- rewind: telling *(assumed)*
- seal: closed *(assumed)*
- span: serial
- timelines: the main timeline is permanent and its consequences are real. Forks and holodeck runs are an escape hatch for fun that doesn't wreck my career or reputation: `// save`, `// fork`, `// holodeck`, `// return` *(assumed: main permanent, forks free)*.
- evaluation: every watch is scored against modules/eval/GOALS.md. The evaluation is on the GM, not just me. It's a prototype that gets refined through use.
