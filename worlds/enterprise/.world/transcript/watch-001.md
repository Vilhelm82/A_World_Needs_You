# Watch 001 — transcript

## turn 1

Main Engineering, a little after the start of alpha shift. The warp core's slow blue pulse climbs the chamber behind the master systems display, and an ensign at the far console is reading a diagnostic aloud to nobody in particular.

Lieutenant Commander MacDougal has the display lit before you reach it. No good morning.

"Docking latch four, sir. Ventral interface, frame thirty-one." She touches the table and twelve strain traces unroll across it, one per latch. Eleven run flat. The fourth spikes, holds, and comes back down. "That's the separation, running from Q. Warp nine and a half, past the red line, which the book says you don't do at any warp. Eleven of them took it. Number four carried half again its rated load for four-tenths of a second."

She lays a second set beside the first. Twelve flat lines.

"And that's your docking at Deneb. Seated, nominal, every sensor happy." She folds her arms. "The sensors are inside the latch mechanism. The actuator housing is round them. If the housing's cracked, they're sitting in the crack telling me everything's lovely."

Over her shoulder: "Ensign, run four again from the top." The ensign does. The same spike climbs the table.

"I can't see that housing with the saucer on. I want a controlled separation at sublight. Six hours, my people out on the ventral hull with a structural imager, and then you put her back the way you did at Deneb." A breath. "I'd like it on the schedule before the captain reads my report. So it lands as a plan, not a complaint."

At her elbow, on a napkin on the console, a replicated scone with one bite out of it.

## turn 2

MacDougal looks at you a moment longer than the order needs. Then at the scone.

"Procedure by eleven hundred." She picks it up, turns it over like a part that failed inspection, and takes a bite. Chews. "My grandmother's. The recipe says a good handful of butter. The replicator wants a number, and every number I give it is wrong." She puts it down. "Order obeyed, sir."

She's already back at the display. "Ensign, pull the full separation checklist from the shakedown and put it on my padd. All of it, not the summary."

"Aye, Commander."

To you: "I'll want the battle bridge crewed for it, and somebody on the saucer who knows what a docking feels like from the inside. If you're flying her back, that's you." A pause. "I'll put six hours in the procedure. I'd rather you didn't let him talk me down to four."

## turn 3

She doesn't pretend not to understand you.

"Four." She draws it on the display with a finger as she goes. Separation and stand-off, half an hour. Two suited teams of three on the twelve housings, twenty minutes a housing: two hours. Your approach from five hundred metres at half a metre a second, seventeen minutes and whatever the alignment costs you. An hour re-mating the interface and running the checks. "If I looked at number four and nothing else, two and a half. I won't look at number four and nothing else. If it took the load sideways, its neighbours felt it."

"Seven on the hull. Two teams and me." A glance at the ensign, back to you. "The other two hours are for when the imager tells me something I don't like and I want to look again. They're real. They're just not required."

## turn 4

"Forty." MacDougal weighs that. "She had big hands."

But she's writing on the padd the ensign has brought her, and from where you stand you can read the line going in under step nine: HOLD POINT - CHIEF ENGINEER'S CALL.

"Then the extension goes in as a hold point, not a request. If I stop, the clock stops, and nobody on the battle bridge asks me why on an open channel." She doesn't look up. "The recipe's in her handwriting. I'll copy it out for you. The book stays in my quarters."

You're halfway to the doors when your combadge chirps.

"Yar to Commander Lloyd." Brisk; tactical noise behind her. "When you've got ten minutes, I need them. Intruder response. Q walked onto the bridge, and half my people found out from the viewscreen."

## turn 5

Behind you, MacDougal, not looking up from the padd: "She'd have liked you, sir. She'd also have argued."

The doors close on the warp core.

The turbolift lets you out onto the bridge. La Forge, at conn, half turns: "Morning, Commander." Data doesn't look up from ops; his hands are moving through a calibration sequence at a speed nobody else on the bridge bothers to watch any more. Troi, in her seat beside the captain's empty chair, glances at you and back at the viewscreen.

Yar is waiting at tactical with a padd already held out.

"Incident log. Every time Q was aboard." You take it. Three entries for the bridge: the barrier, where he froze Lieutenant Torres with his phaser still in his hand, and twice more near Deneb IV. One for the courtroom. Under each, the same line in her terse hand: *No intruder alert sounded. No security team dispatched.*

"The names are on the second page. Eleven of my twenty-three on watch. They found out we had an intruder on the bridge when they saw him on the feed at their posts." She keeps her voice down; the bridge is small. "I'm not asking to shoot at him. The captain made the call not to, and he was right. I'm asking that when somebody nobody can account for walks onto this bridge, my people hear it from me and not from a display."

## turn 6

"Then I'd want somebody to notice." Yar says it flatly, but she's already pulling the padd back from you and writing. "Silent to my watch. Thirty seconds. Yellow on its own if nobody answers." She stops. "If he freezes the whole bridge, nobody answers, and it goes. Good. If he's polite and says the word himself?"

From ops, without turning round: "The stand-down could require a voice-print match against the bridge officers on duty, Lieutenant." Data swivels. "Forgive me. The bridge is acoustically small. Internal sensors can be set to flag any life sign on this deck that matches no crew record or authorised visitor. I could write that filter after the calibration on beta shift." A slight pause. "I should say that no one has examined what the internal sensors recorded during Q's appearances. I do not know whether such a filter would register him."

Yar looks at him a moment, then at you. "It'll fire every time somebody brings a guest up here."

"Then someone acknowledges it," Data says.

"Then someone acknowledges it." She finishes the line and turns the padd so you can read it. "It changes the ship's alert condition on its own, so it's the captain's to sign. I'll have it written up tonight. You want to carry it in, or do I?"

## turn 7

"Logged before they set foot. Understood." Yar takes the padd back. "Mine, then. Tonight."

Data has already turned to his console. "Aligning the optical record with the internal sensor logs at matching timestamps." Readouts slide across ops, too fast to follow: the bridge from the forward camera, Q in his robes beside the conn, and beneath it the life-sign plot for the same deck, the same seconds. Troi has turned in her seat to watch.

Thirty seconds, perhaps.

"Commander." Data sounds, if anything, interested. "Three appearances on the bridge. In each, the optical record shows him for the whole interval. The internal sensors logged a life sign for every crew member present, and none at his position. Not a weak reading. None." He tilts his head. "That is what these sensors recorded at their routine settings. It does not establish that he cannot be detected by other means. But a filter that relies on life signs alone would not have fired."

He looks at Yar. "A second trigger, then. The optical record: an unidentified figure on the bridge feed."

At conn, La Forge has stopped pretending not to listen. "So he was standing right there and the ship said nobody was."

## turn 8

Data considers it for exactly as long as it takes him. "A constant spectral model of the bridge, reconciled against every accounted presence. Feasible. It will flag a great deal at first. Lighting changes, a padd left on a console." A small pause. "And it must count me as accounted for. I do not register as a life sign either."

Yar, writing: "Put that in the first paragraph."

La Forge has turned his chair all the way round now. "Both times, yeah. I'd just beamed back with Lieutenant Yar and the Counselor. Worf had the helm, so I was at the aft stations." He taps the VISOR's edge, absently. "Here's the thing. People through this are busy. Your face is warmer than your hands, your pulse is going in your throat, there's a sort of flicker all over, nerves, I guess. Never stops." He shakes his head. "Him? Same temperature everywhere. Face, hands, all of it. No pulse I could see. No flicker. Like somebody'd painted a man to look warm."

"Why did you not mention it?" Data asks.

"Who was I going to mention it to? It was Q." La Forge spreads his hands. "And I'd been aboard about an hour. I figured maybe it was me."

## turn 9

"Bodies." Data inclines his head. "Understood." Then, because he is Data: "One refinement, Commander. During the second appearance near Deneb IV he sat in the captain's chair for some time without moving. A body, still, would also need to be counted."

La Forge is grinning back at you, which on him takes up most of his face. "Every time, aye, sir." He swings back to conn. "You might regret that. I see a lot."

And then, without a sound, close as a hand on your arm and nowhere near your ears:

*Will. Later, off duty. We should talk.*

Troi hasn't moved. She's looking at the viewscreen, and the stars streaming past it at warp five.

## turn 10

La Forge opens his mouth to answer and stops. Behind the VISOR, nothing of his eyes shows, but his head goes very slightly to one side.

"Huh." He turns the chair back round. "Yeah. Same. The robes and the uniform. I didn't think about it." He's working it through as he goes. "That's wrong. The outside of anything you're wearing's always cooler than you are. Air's taking heat off it. You can see where a collar sits, where a sleeve ends. On him there wasn't any edge to see. Face, cloth, hands, one temperature."

Data has turned from ops. "Then whatever presented itself as clothing was not losing heat to the room as fabric would." He pauses. "Or it was, and was being kept at the same temperature as the rest of him."

"Or the VISOR saw what he wanted it to see," Yar says, still writing.

Nobody answers that.

## turn 11

"Yes, Commander. With a qualification." Data brings up a schematic of the bridge on his console: the overhead pickups, the environmental sensors in the ceiling ring. "The optical pickups see in the visible and the near infrared. Body heat is radiated much further out, around nine micrometres. The environmental system does map temperature across the bridge, but in patches a metre across. It would tell you the room was warm, not that a collar was the same temperature as a face."

"So we need real thermal pickups," La Forge says.

"Dedicated long-wave pickups, yes. The optics are not demanding. An aperture of about a centimetre resolves a centimetre at ten metres at those wavelengths." Data glances at La Forge. "It is an installation. Engineering's."

"I'm off at sixteen hundred." La Forge doesn't quite look round. "If the chief'll have me."

Data turns back to you. "I should note the basis. If a thermal image would have shown him, it is because Lieutenant La Forge remembers him as warm. That is one recollection, by one instrument, a day after the event. It is worth fitting the pickups. It is not yet evidence that they would work."

## turn 12

"Default." Yar says it the way she'd sign something. "Good. That goes in the first line, ahead of him." She strikes something out on the padd and writes over it.

"A cloaked boarder, a transporter intrusion, a shapeshifter," Data says. "Each would present to at least one of the two triggers. The thermal pickups improve the protocol against all of them." He returns to his calibration.

La Forge taps his combadge. "La Forge to Commander MacDougal."

A pause long enough to hear her finish a sentence to someone else. *"MacDougal."*

"Ma'am, the first officer's got me fitting thermal pickups on the bridge, my off-duty hours, starting sixteen hundred. He said clear it with you first."

*"Did he."* Another pause. *"Sixteen hundred. Bring your own tools, sign for anything you take out of stores, and if the latch-four procedure needs hands, it comes first."*

"Yes, ma'am."

*"MacDougal out."*

La Forge looks back at you, delighted, and doesn't say anything at all.
