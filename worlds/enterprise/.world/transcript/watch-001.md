# Watch 001 — transcript

## turn 1

Main Engineering, a little after the start of alpha shift. The warp core's slow blue pulse climbs the chamber behind the master systems display, and an ensign at the far console is reading a diagnostic aloud to nobody in particular.

Lieutenant Commander MacDougal has the display lit before you reach it. No good morning.

"Docking latch four, sir. Ventral interface, frame thirty-one." She touches the table and twelve strain traces unroll across it, one per latch. Eleven run flat. The fourth spikes, holds, and comes back down. "That's the separation, running from Q. Warp nine and a half, past the red line, which the book says you don't do at any warp. Eleven of them took it. Number four carried half again its rated load for four-tenths of a second."

She lays a second set beside the first. Twelve flat lines.

"And that's your docking at Deneb. Seated, nominal, every sensor happy." She folds her arms. "The sensors are inside the latch mechanism. The actuator housing is round them. If the housing's cracked, they're sitting in the crack telling me everything's lovely."

Over her shoulder: "Ensign, run four again from the top." The ensign does. The same spike climbs the table.

"I can't see that housing with the saucer on. I want a controlled separation at sublight. Six hours, my people out on the ventral hull with a structural imager, and then you put her back the way you did at Deneb." A breath. "I'd like it on the schedule before the captain reads my report. So it lands as a plan, not a complaint."

At her elbow, on a napkin on the console, a replicated scone with one bite out of it.

## turn 2

MacDougal looks at you a moment longer than the order needs. Then at the scone.

"Procedure by eleven hundred." She picks it up, turns it over like a part that failed inspection, and takes a bite. Chews. "My grandmother's. The recipe says a good handful of butter. The replicator wants a number, and every number I give it is wrong." She puts it down. "Order obeyed, sir."

She's already back at the display. "Ensign, pull the full separation checklist from the shakedown and put it on my padd. All of it, not the summary."

"Aye, Commander."

To you: "I'll want the battle bridge crewed for it, and somebody on the saucer who knows what a docking feels like from the inside. If you're flying her back, that's you." A pause. "I'll put six hours in the procedure. I'd rather you didn't let him talk me down to four."

## turn 3

She doesn't pretend not to understand you.

"Four." She draws it on the display with a finger as she goes. Separation and stand-off, half an hour. Two suited teams of three on the twelve housings, twenty minutes a housing: two hours. Your approach from five hundred metres at half a metre a second, seventeen minutes and whatever the alignment costs you. An hour re-mating the interface and running the checks. "If I looked at number four and nothing else, two and a half. I won't look at number four and nothing else. If it took the load sideways, its neighbours felt it."

"Seven on the hull. Two teams and me." A glance at the ensign, back to you. "The other two hours are for when the imager tells me something I don't like and I want to look again. They're real. They're just not required."

## turn 4

"Forty." MacDougal weighs that. "She had big hands."

But she's writing on the padd the ensign has brought her, and from where you stand you can read the line going in under step nine: HOLD POINT - CHIEF ENGINEER'S CALL.

"Then the extension goes in as a hold point, not a request. If I stop, the clock stops, and nobody on the battle bridge asks me why on an open channel." She doesn't look up. "The recipe's in her handwriting. I'll copy it out for you. The book stays in my quarters."

You're halfway to the doors when your combadge chirps.

"Yar to Commander Lloyd." Brisk; tactical noise behind her. "When you've got ten minutes, I need them. Intruder response. Q walked onto the bridge, and half my people found out from the viewscreen."

## turn 5

Behind you, MacDougal, not looking up from the padd: "She'd have liked you, sir. She'd also have argued."

The doors close on the warp core.

The turbolift lets you out onto the bridge. La Forge, at conn, half turns: "Morning, Commander." Data doesn't look up from ops; his hands are moving through a calibration sequence at a speed nobody else on the bridge bothers to watch any more. Troi, in her seat beside the captain's empty chair, glances at you and back at the viewscreen.

Yar is waiting at tactical with a padd already held out.

"Incident log. Every time Q was aboard." You take it. Three entries for the bridge: the barrier, where he froze Lieutenant Torres with his phaser still in his hand, and twice more near Deneb IV. One for the courtroom. Under each, the same line in her terse hand: *No intruder alert sounded. No security team dispatched.*

"The names are on the second page. Eleven of my twenty-three on watch. They found out we had an intruder on the bridge when they saw him on the feed at their posts." She keeps her voice down; the bridge is small. "I'm not asking to shoot at him. The captain made the call not to, and he was right. I'm asking that when somebody nobody can account for walks onto this bridge, my people hear it from me and not from a display."
