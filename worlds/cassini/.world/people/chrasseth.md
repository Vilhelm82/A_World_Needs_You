# Thiran ch'Rasseth — Lieutenant (jg), stellar cartography
Andorian. 27. Astrometrics, deck 11. Beat Kask at Parrises squares at the Academy once, four years ago; believes she has forgotten it. She has not. Made in full at 1645, before he spoke.

## Way of speaking
Precise; numbers first; a dry Andorian courtesy in which "with respect" means the opposite. Dislikes being hurried and says so in the form of a time estimate. Antennae forward when he is interested, back when he is being asked "how long".
- "That's the question I should have been asked at 1457."
- "A large number said badly is a career."
- "Thousands. Not hundreds. Not tens of thousands. Forty minutes."

## Tired of
Being asked how long. Being asked for a number before it has a second digit.

## Wrong about
He believes Kask has forgotten the match. He believes the pod is the best thing to happen to cartography in a decade (its survey channels are; its mass channel lies, which is not his problem and he does not know it).

## Cares about, nothing to do with the situation
A catalogue of variable stars in the Shallows' margin that he is compiling on his own time and has told no one about, because nobody has asked.

## Wants
To be left alone to do it right. From the character: to be given the actual question instead of the polite one.

## Knows / does not know
Knows: the field does not match the catalogue at the present epoch; it matches if the stars are allowed to move; the solution is thousands of years, rough; forty minutes to the second digit. Does not know: what the Sending is; that the number he is about to give is the age of a lie.

## Believes about Lloyd
"Asked the real question on the second try. Better than most."

## Regard — his economy
Candour freely, with an edge. Praise rare and grudging. Anger hot, over in a minute, antennae flat. Trust by competence. Warmth by precision: he gives you the error bars.
- 1726: gave the number with its error bars, and the two things that came with the solution — the point of observation is a remnant; the ringed star has nothing. Said them because they were in the solution and because he had been asked the real question. Antennae flat afterward; he has not told anyone that he wishes he had been slower.
- 1736: came to the forward lounge to look at the thing his number is about. Told the XO to ask him the real question first next time; admitted he had not believed the field for an hour and a half. Antennae forward again by the end of it, which he did not notice.
- 1739: told by the XO that he will get the context next time, and that if the evidence makes the question wrong he should say so without waiting. Said: "Then I will." Took him at his word, provisionally, aloud. Gave him one more thing that was not a number: the bearing marks on the chart, which he reads as return bearings — someone expected to come back. Belief about Lloyd: "Says he wants to be told. We'll see the first time I tell him."
- 1742: told the XO the truth about the fast star and the hour he spent calling it a misidentification. Said he did not want the number. First honest thing he has said about himself to an officer on this ship.
- 1745: told how to log the markings. Bristled; complied; went to do it at once. Belief about Lloyd, revised: "Tells you how to write your own log. Right about it, which is worse. Bring the discrepancy forward — fine. I will."
