# Episode — how a situation opens, runs and closes

A world under this preset is `span: serial`: situations close, the world goes on. An episode is one situation. It may take one sitting or several. The standing crew and the ship persist; the situation does not.

## At every open
1. Read the charter, `state.md`, the tail of the ledger, and the `episode` block of `truth.json`.
2. If the episode is **open**: take the world's own turn — movers step, pressures move by their causes, one consequence matures, each person revises what they believe from what they witnessed — and continue. No recap. Someone is already talking.
3. If the episode is **closed** (the last record says so): build the next one before anyone speaks. Write six candidate situations with nothing in common — none a canon plot, none already in `.world/build.md` — roll a die from the shell, take what falls, run the two passes (*Particular*, *Sound*), and write the new `episode` block beneath the standing crew. Then take the world's own turn for the interval since the close: days or weeks pass between episodes; the crew have been doing their jobs; what the last episode set in motion has moved. Then open, in the world, already moving.

## An episode's truth
`truth.json` → `episode` holds the situation in layers; the episode's own people (made in full, in `.world/people/`); the sources that lie and why; three doors per secret, of different kinds; several resolutions with costs, one of them nobody proposes; pressures as processes; movers; distance per resolution, descriptive only; `in_motion`. The standing crew's per-episode wants go in the episode block; who they are stays in their files.

## The teaser
An episode opens the way the show did: in the middle of something small, with the situation arriving inside the first exchange. No recap of the last episode. If the crew refer to it, they refer to it as people do — a name, a grudge, a thing that broke.

## Closing
An episode closes when its situation resolves or is lost, in the world, by the world's logic. Whoever holds the thread says what changed and which decisions were the character's. Picard writes the log. The stand-down record marks the episode `closed` and carries into `in_motion` whatever the ending set going: a reply from Starfleet, a consequence maturing, a person's changed regard, a thread nobody pulled. The next episode grows from that and from what the crew was already doing. Never from what the person seemed to enjoy.

## The captain's evaluation
Where the charter puts the character under a captain's evaluation, it is a fact of the world with a paper trail — a selection to confirm, an endorsement to write — and the captain's regard moves on decisions only. It is never announced, scored or resolved as a test. It surfaces as a captain does it: a question in the ready room, a silence, a name on a report the character may or may not be shown. Across episodes it accumulates.
