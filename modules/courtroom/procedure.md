# Courtroom procedure v1.0

**Authority label: SIMULATION SIMPLIFICATION throughout.** These are complete
house rules for a fictional NSW-style civil hearing, not a statement of the
Evidence Act, court rules or current professional obligations. Case-specific
contract terms and agreed facts are labelled CASE STIPULATION in the brief.
Address the presiding judicial officer as Your Honour. Use plaintiff/defendant,
not prosecution/accused. There is no jury, criminal offence or sentencing.

## P1. Scope, issues and burden
The plaintiff must establish each required element on the balance of
probabilities: more likely than not on the available admissible record. Do not
turn this into a displayed percentage or demand proof beyond reasonable doubt.
The defendant need not prove a different history just to contest an element.
The plaintiff bears the amount claimed as well as the basis of entitlement.
The brief identifies I1 and I2 and the available outcomes. A private author's
answer is not evidence. A tie or unresolved essential element is not proved.

## P2. Order of hearing
Preparation and client conference; preliminary agreed facts and document issues;
short openings; plaintiff's witnesses W1 and W2; defendant's witnesses W3 and W4;
closings, with a brief plaintiff reply limited to new matters; reasoned judgment.
Counsel may seek a different order, recess, a witness recall, a stipulation or
permission to reopen on an identified basis. Neither party has a fixed timer.
Witnesses are outside the hearing until called unless the record says otherwise.
Each side has exchanged E01-E10 and WS1-WS4. This is disclosure, not blanket
admission. E01 alone is stipulated admitted for its contractual terms.

## P3. Questions and interruption
Ask one intelligible material question at a time. Leading questions are allowed
on cross-examination; on examination-in-chief they are restricted to preliminary
or genuinely undisputed matters. A yes/no question does not compel a misleading
answer. A witness may correct a false premise or explain a necessary qualification.
The other side gets an objection opportunity before a material answer. Routine
uncontested questioning can be compressed only with the player's permission.
A request for a concession is not an admission; the witness's actual answer is.

## P4. Objections
Recognised grounds: relevance; lack of personal knowledge/speculation; hearsay;
leading on a disputed issue in chief; compound or unintelligible question;
argumentative/harassing repetition; assumes an unestablished fact; misstates the
record; opinion beyond the witness's basis; missing document foundation;
privileged/confidential material; and use beyond an admission's limitation.
State the ground in plain language or by rule. Pause the answer. Hear a concise
reply, then sustain, overrule or require rephrasing, with a short reason.
An objection is not evidence. A sustained question is withdrawn, not answered.
A proper factual hypothesis may be put to a witness without pretending it is
already proved. Clarify its status rather than banning all disputed questions.

## P5. Documents, authenticity and permissible use
Use the fixed document IDs. Tender a document by ID and state its purpose.
A document can be disclosed, marked, admitted, limited or excluded. A limited
admission names its permitted uses: truth, notice, context or credibility.
Authenticity means it is the described document, not that every statement is true.
A document may be authenticated through its author/custodian, a stipulation or
its supplied provenance. A displayed document is not automatically admitted.
The bench may inspect a disputed document solely for the admissibility ruling;
it may not then use excluded content on the merits. WS statements become merits
evidence only when adopted in testimony and admitted, not merely exchanged.

## P6. Hearsay and opinion
An out-of-court assertion offered for its truth needs a stated basis. For this
case, permit: an adopted first-hand statement with its maker available for cross;
a routine contemporaneous business entry authenticated by its maker/custodian;
an opposing party's authorised statement when offered against that party; or
an explicit stipulation. These are house-rule categories, not exhaustive law.
Other uses, such as notice or explaining a later action, may receive limited
admission. Second-hand repetition does not create an independent source.
Ordinary descriptions from perception are allowed. Technical conclusions need
an identified foundation; nobody becomes an expert because counsel says so.

## P7. Prior statements and challenge
Identify the document and exact passage, establish authorship/adoption, and put
the alleged inconsistency fairly. Give the witness a chance to answer. Distinguish
an omission from a contradiction. A genuine correction is not automatically a lie.
A material allegation about a witness's conduct should be put before relying on
it in closing; a failure can lead to recall or reduced reliance, not automatic loss.
The phrase 'you opened the door' creates no entitlement. Identify the specific
fairness or admission issue and obtain a ruling under these disclosed rules.

## P8. Inference and weight
Consider circumstantial evidence together. Separate observations from inference,
intent from provenance, access from use, and disbelief from affirmative proof.
Related effects can support a pattern without counting as independent sources.
Neither a charitable explanation nor an accusatory explanation gets preference
merely for its moral tone. Compare the actual evidential support and constraints.
Demeanour is not a lie detector. Confidence, repetition and eloquence are not facts.
Evidence of cost or motive is not proof that the predicted action occurred.

## P9. Scope of admissions and concessions
Distinguish a party's factual concession, counsel's hypothetical, the witness's
answer and the bench's finding. Record the exact scope. Agreement to authenticity
does not concede a document's truth; knowledge of an act does not concede intent.
A concession cannot be silently expanded in a later summary. Counsel may clarify
or seek to withdraw one openly, with the other side heard on any prejudice.

## P10. Judgment and remedies
Resolve I1 (written authorisation) and, where necessary, I2 (net additional amount).
Possible orders: dismiss the additional-charge claim, allow it in part, or allow
it up to AUD 24,600. No punitive award, criminal finding, separate restitution
claim, interest or costs is in scope. Cite admitted documents with permitted uses
and exact testimony IDs. Address the strongest material competing interpretation.
If I1 fails, I2 can be not reached. A win is not an advocacy score.

## P11. Missing material and errors
No retroactive historical facts, new decisive documents or tailored recollections.
A new calculation can use fixed admitted inputs, with workings available to both
sides. If a genuinely material authoring gap arises, record it and pause that issue.
Do not convert an engine error into witness dishonesty. Correct it openly, mark the
original turn, and repair affected opportunities or rulings. Historical case files
never change during a live trial. A broken segment is not a valid assessment.

## P12. Assistance, privacy and completion
Procedural help is available on request without giving the substantive answer.
Client discussions and `// prepare` stay private. Opposing counsel reasons from
its own packet plus disclosed events. The bench decides from its authorised
record. A ruling on an issue outside P1-P12 is paused; any agreed new simulation
rule is disclosed to both sides prospectively and versioned, never retroactive.
Conclude when the case is ready for decision. Do not extend it to create a twist.
